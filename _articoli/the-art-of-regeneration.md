intro: >-
  Every word has a beginning and an end.


  And yet, there is one word that has a beginning but doesn’t truly end with its final vowel.


  Change.


  Beyond that final sound lies an entire world.

  There is uncertainty, but also an infinity of possibilities.

  Including the possibility of finding the right people and the right place.


  Some people see change as something that gives new energy to life.


  Dr. Alessandra D’Agostino is one of those “let’s do it,” “we never stop” kind of people.

  And we couldn’t help but talk to her about exactly that: The Art of Regeneration.

      Others, like Alessandra, see it as a new beginning.


      She began her career as an aesthetic doctor over 20 years ago in her hometown, Naples. But she could never have imagined that an opportunity would come her way and change her life completely: the Middle East.


      In 2016, Alessandra moved to the Kingdom of Bahrain.

      Alone.


      For two years, she practised alongside Arab colleagues.

      It was an experience that changed her life.

      Her expertise was enriched by international perspectives, while the experience strengthened her determination and courage.

      In Salerno, she opened her aesthetic medicine clinic, which today has
      become a trusted point of reference for her patients.


      Change is also a word deeply connected to the world of aesthetic medicine.


      Our skin changes every day.

      It tells the story of what we have lived through.


      At this point, someone might say: “People who turn to aesthetic medicine don’t accept themselves,” or “They should seek psychological help instead.”


      If we are talking about true aesthetic medicine, however, that debate doesn’t really apply.


      If we are talking about those who promote dramatically altering facial features, or those who advocate volume at all costs, then yes.


      Fortunately, we are talking about the first kind.


      Alessandra’s philosophy is to “soften the passage of time.”

      So much so that it has become her signature phrase: “The doc who softens time.”


      And the art of regeneration can also be found in her hands.


      According to her experience and her approach, an aesthetic medicine procedure is not necessarily something negative.
